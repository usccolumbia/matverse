# Additional perspective papers checked

Four reference PDFs were present in the workspace during the final coverage review. Their SHA-256 receipts are in [the reference manifest](../research/provenance/workspace_reference_receipts.json). The PDFs remain local and are not part of this repository. The requested ACS paper has its own [crosswalk](review-crosswalk.md).

| Reference | Material inspected | Contribution to the coverage check |
|---|---|---|
| Mishra et al., [The rise of the digital materials ecosystem](https://doi.org/10.20517/aiagent.2026.08), *AI Agent* 2, 6 (2026) | Five-page research highlight | Describes connected databases, models, theory and laboratory feedback, with DigCat, DDSE/DigBat, DigHyd and DigMat as examples. It is a conceptual highlight; its platform mentions do not establish independent dataset counts. |
| Zhang et al., [Accelerating Catalyst Materials Discovery With Large Artificial Intelligence Models](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202526150), *Angewandte Chemie International Edition* 65, e26150 (2026) | Minireview, including database/MLIP discussion | Connects catalyst databases, atomistic potentials and language models in automated workflows. Used as a check on the surfaces/catalysis and mixed-domain training-data coverage, with original releases retained as size evidence. |
| Xin et al., [Roadmap for transforming heterogeneous catalysis with artificial intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-026-01479-x), *Nature Catalysis* 9, 102–111 (2026) | Abstract, metadata and public reference list only; the supplied printout is a subscription preview | Identifies data availability/quality, transferability and the simulation-to-experiment gap as issues for catalysis AI. No claims about the inaccessible full text or dataset sizes are inferred. |

These perspectives motivate the comparison dimensions; they are not empirical validations of every dataset they mention. Their overlapping author groups and citation chains should not be treated as independent confirmation of numerical claims.
